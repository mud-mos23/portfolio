I built the timesheet system in [Bubely ERP](https://github.com/mud-mos23/bubely-erp). The challenge: everyone hates filling out timesheets.

## Why They're Hated

- **Retrospective tracking** — "What did I do 3 days ago?" is hard to answer
- **Billing vs reality** — tracking to the 6-minute increment is absurd for knowledge work
- **Punitive culture** — timesheets used for monitoring rather than planning

## A Better Model

Bubely's approach:

1. **Project-level time budgets** — track at the project level, not the task level
2. **Daily prompts** — a simple "how many hours on each project today?" (not "what did you do at 10:37 AM?")
3. **Rounded increments** — 15-minute minimum, not 6-minute
4. **No approval workflow** — trust-based, with anomaly detection for outliers

## Results

In testing, this reduced time-entry friction by 60% while improving accuracy. People are more honest when they're not being watched at the minute level.

[The Timesheet Hell research paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220591216_Time_Sheets_What_Are_They_Good_For) confirms that detailed timesheets actually reduce accuracy because people estimate rather than track.

The best timesheet system is one people don't hate using.
