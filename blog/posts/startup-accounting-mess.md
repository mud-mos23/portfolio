I've seen startup accounting in three stages:

## Stage 1: The Spreadsheet Era (0-10 employees)

Revenue, expenses, and runway tracked in a single Google Sheet. It works until it doesn't — usually when an investor asks for a balance sheet and your "accounts" don't reconcile.

## Stage 2: The Band-Aid Era (10-50 employees)

QuickBooks or Xero for core accounting, spreadsheets for everything else. The problem: nothing integrates. Payroll data sits separately from expenses, which sit separately from revenue.

## Stage 3: The ERP Era (50+ employees)

This is where [Bubely ERP](https://github.com/mud-mos23/bubely-erp) and similar systems add value — unified financial data with consistent reporting.

## The Real Fix

You don't need an ERP at Stage 1. But you need **structure**:
- Chart of accounts designed from day 1
- Monthly reconciliation as a non-negotiable process
- Separate personal and business finances

[The Y Combinator guide to startup finance](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7n-how-to-manage-startup-finances) recommends hiring a fractional CFO earlier than founders expect.

The mess isn't about tools — it's about treating accounting as an afterthought until it's an emergency.
